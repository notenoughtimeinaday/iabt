# Insured Spending Origin and Pooled Layout

## Why this belongs in IABT

IABT was not conceived merely as a page builder. A foundational use case was the user's **Pooled Layout** for Insured Spending: a transparent, rule-driven system where many members make small contributions so that a member can receive a larger purchase benefit or financial assist. The difficulty of expressing this coordinated financial logic in existing builders helped motivate IABT itself.

## Product role

The Pooled Layout should become a flagship IABT template and proving ground for the larger platform. It tests whether IABT can create more than pages:

- contribution-ledger prototypes;
- eligibility and selection rules;
- member states and recurring workflows;
- notices, merchant flows, and administration;
- simulations, audit trails, and explainable outcomes;
- integrations with approved providers.

IABT should let a user describe an outcome, connect the accounts they authorize, approve consequential actions, and allow the platform to plan, build, test, and package the result. It should guide connector authorization, secret setup, test-mode provisioning, verification, recovery, and deployment so future users do not repeat the original manual setup burden.

## Financial-safety boundary

The canonical IABT template is a **no-real-money prototype and simulation** until the economic model has been classified by qualified counsel and implemented through appropriate licensed partners. Depending on final mechanics, pooled funds may implicate insurance, money transmission, lending, sweepstakes, cooperative or mutual-aid rules, banking, tax, consumer-protection, and securities requirements.

IABT may model funds flow, member rules, ledgers, and provider interfaces. It must not present an unclassified prototype as an insurance company or autonomously hold, move, promise, or award real funds.

## Preserved design questions

The following remain deliberate product decisions rather than assumptions:

- contribution amount and frequency;
- pool size and benefit amount;
- whether selection is rotating, need-based, randomized, merchant-funded, refundable, or guaranteed;
- member eligibility, cancellation, refund, and dispute rules;
- ownership and custody of funds;
- jurisdiction and licensed-provider structure.

## Long-term direction

A strong implementation separates the customer experience from regulated rails: Insured Spending owns the experience, rules engine, analytics, transparency, and brand, while qualified financial, payment, banking, or insurance partners provide any regulated infrastructure.
